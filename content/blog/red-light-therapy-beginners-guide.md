## What Is Red Light Therapy?

Red light therapy is a non-invasive wellness technology that uses specific wavelengths of LED light to gently energize the cells in your skin. It's also known as **photobiomodulation (PBM)** or **low-level light therapy (LLLT)**.

Unlike UV light (which can damage skin over time), red and near-infrared light work at a cellular level to support your body's natural processes. The therapy is **non-thermal** — meaning it generates no heat — and is gentle enough for daily use on all skin types.

This technology has been used for decades in clinical and professional settings. Today, it's available in convenient home devices that make daily sessions practical and affordable.

> *"Red light therapy is one of the most promising non-invasive wellness technologies available today. Its applications continue to expand as research deepens."* — Dr. Michael Hamblin, Harvard Medical School (former associate professor, well-known photobiomodulation researcher)

## How Does It Work?

At its simplest level, red light therapy works by delivering specific wavelengths of light to the skin's surface and deeper tissue layers.

The **mitochondria** — the energy centers of your cells — absorb this light energy and use it to produce more **adenosine triphosphate (ATP)**, which is essentially fuel for your cells. When cells have more energy, they can function more efficiently.

The most commonly used wavelengths in home devices are:

| Wavelength | Type | Properties |
|------------|------|------------|
| **630–660nm** | Red light | Works at the skin surface level |
| **830–850nm** | Near-infrared (NIR) | Penetrates deeper into tissue |

Most quality home devices combine both red and near-infrared wavelengths to deliver a more complete experience.

## What to Look For in a Home Device

If you're considering adding a red light therapy device to your wellness routine, here are the key factors to evaluate:

**Wavelengths matter.** Look for devices that include both red (630-660nm) and near-infrared (830-850nm) wavelengths. This combination provides the most comprehensive coverage.

**Build quality.** The device should be made from skin-friendly, durable materials. Medical-grade silicone is a common choice for mask-style devices.

**Ease of use.** The best device is the one you'll actually use every day. Look for features like automatic timers, rechargeable batteries, and hands-free design.

**Price vs. value.** Home devices range from $60 to $400+. The sweet spot is a well-built device that uses the right wavelengths without the premium markup of luxury brands.
